Understanding the Risks of Bionic Technology

While bionic integration offers remarkable benefits, it also introduces significant risks that society must carefully consider and manage. Even though the bionic future looks bright with improving lifestyles and enhancing performances, it is not always as simple as it seems. There are already a lot of questions being raised during the development of new innovative bionics such as human rights, personal identity, and societal equity. All of these risks are relevant to both civilian and military settings and must be addressed because they are unethical.

Critical Risk Areas

Cybersecurity Threats

As bionic devices become more connected, they create potential entry points for cyber attacks. A compromised neural interface could allow hackers to hijack limb movements or access sensitive neural data. Recent studies show 23% of medical IoT devices have critical vulnerabilities. That being said, can we rely on government issued prothestics to uphold there resposbilities. With corruption within politics, can we be sure to trust the data put into the bionics are not being dispatched without our knowledge.

Health and Safety Risks

Surgical procedures for advanced bionic integration carry inherent risks including infection (occurring in <8% of cases) and nerve damage. Long-term effects of neural implants remain poorly understood with some patients reporting chronic pain or unexpected side effects years after implantation. With safety concerns towards the likes of this, how can we expect to supply third-world countires with bionic technogoly given there accessbility to proper equipment and surgons.

Social Implications

The potential for "bionic divides" between enhanced and non-enhanced individuals raises profound social questions. Could we see discrimination against those who choose not to enhance? The biggest problem for everyday people is that bionic limbs are expensive especially when it's integrated with high-tech things like neural feedback. Making it so that only those who are rich or have advanced health insurance would be able to have access to these products. As noted in the Bionics Global Market Report 2025, bionics market dispersion reveals the difference in availability between low income and high income regions. This phenomenon can create something like “bio-divide” where there will be a gap between people who couldn't afford the enhancements and people who could. This could lead to discrimination to the people with enhancements.

Might this technology exacerbate existing inequalities when access remains limited to wealthy nations? These are questions that need to be addressed before the technology is widely adopted for this could ultimately cause more societal issues globally. As the bionic development is accelerating, the work around the social, ethical aspects, and public policy should also be developed to prevent inequitable access, not being able to maintain human agency and society division.

Military Concerns

From a military perspective, the most concerning problem is the loss of human autonomy. This is discussed in a study by Hagos and Rawat (2024). Implementation of neuro-symbolic artificial intelligence into military systems could result in a shift in critical decision-making responsibilities from humans to the computer. This would be a problem when we are judging accountability in high-stakes combat scenarios. With brain-machine interfaces implemented could let soldiers control multiple weapons without going through a physical input such as buttons or triggers. If the program acts before soldiers do not have time to assess the full consequences this will alter the soldiers ability to make free choices which would violate long standing military ethics. Furthermore, due to this problem if the military decides to restrict the usage of bionics to certain soldiers or nations it will lead to two levels of soldiers, soldiers with bionics and those without which will raise questions about fairness, pressure to upgrade and affecting military values.
